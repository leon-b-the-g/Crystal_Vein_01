# Kestrel Vane: the vision

Leon's words decide. Lines in quotation marks are his, with the date he said them; a "pick" is an option he chose in a dialog that day. Everything else is the reading agents work from, and it is wrong wherever it disagrees with him. This file changes only from his words (G-03 in [GUARDRAILS](GUARDRAILS.md)). The dated record is [DECISIONS](DECISIONS.md).

## The game in one breath

An electrical engineer wakes on a mining ship, days after something went wrong. The ship is being taken by a living thing that grows, senses and changes. He works his way off it in the dark, with one flashlight and with what he can find, take apart and rebuild. Fixed isometric camera, Unreal Engine 5. Sci-fi action horror.

## The spine

Four lines, in order. When two good ideas pull apart, the higher line wins.

### 1. The place is alive

Pick of 24 Nov, out of four possible leads: the organism leads; its growth is the clock and the threat; the creatures are its hands.

- "the creep is not dynamic enough, it should feel like an organic growth slowly growing around the level." (19 Nov)
- Its life, in his words (20 Nov): "a biological breathing mass, that slowly grows, tests, expands, hardens in established places that it has conquered, and begins to go through its stages of life (sporulation - where it creates clouds of red mist that can travel and infect dead bodies, infestation - where dead bodies reanimate infected with the creep, contagion - intending to spread the creep by attacking other biological beings, crystalisation - the final form of the creep is a hardened/sharp/reflective/still flammable crystal that ends up lining the floor and walls of the areas where it has settled)."
- Of the first stand-in for it (20 Nov): "it does not seem organic, moving or alive".
- **It senses; it does not think.** Pick of 24 Nov: it reacts to what the player does (light, heat, noise, blood) and reads as an animal that can be baited and steered. In Act I nothing it does needs it to know this player in particular.
- **A different sense per form.** Pick of 24 Nov: each stage and each creature has its own sense, and learning which is which is the game. Which form senses what is open (brief [O-001](briefs/O-001-a-sense-per-form.md) proposes it).
- **Burn it back, never beat it.** Pick of 24 Nov: fire buys time and a path; it regrows from where it lives; the pressure never ends. No room is cleansed for good and nothing switches it off.
- **Buds and hosts.** Pick of 24 Nov: small creatures are buds of the mass (its own flesh and crystal given legs, no animal anatomy); large ones are hosts (crew and animals it has remade: anatomy you recognise, overgrown). The older a thing is, the more crystal it carries.

What follows for the work: the organism is the main character of every room, so a brief in any lane says what the organism does in it. A deck gets worse while the player works: pacing comes from its advance, not from more errands.

### 2. The dark and the beam

"The flashlight gimick is only good if 90% lean into it, there should be cues/hints about areas but the actual game should feel like you are stuck in the spacestation/setting, trying to get out and piece it together." (19 Nov)

- Black outside the beam and the lamps. The flashlight is the player's eyes, and it can fail.
- Cues, not orders: one goal, and under it what the player has found out, as facts. Doors say where they lead. The interface never lies and never walks the player through steps.

### 3. Fights are winnable, and they cost

Pick of 24 Nov. The complaint it answers: "enemies didnt feel like a real hazard, easy to kill and no real damage or feedback" (19 Nov).

- One creature can be beaten by timing and position, and it takes something: health, a tool, time, noise that draws more. Groups are deadly.
- Every hit given and every hit taken is felt.
- Not a power fantasy, and not a game of hiding only.

### 4. An engineer's hands

"I would like 4 quick slots and a 9 slot bag, where finding objects feels meaningful and there are regular opportunities to disasemble objects at work benches to make the objects that you want (I want the main character to be an electrical engineer by trade)" (24 Nov)

- Story mode: four quick slots, nine bag slots.
- Few objects, each with a use. No filler loot.
- Workbenches come regularly. Objects are taken apart there and the parts made into what the player wants.
- His answers are an electrician's: wiring, power, light, improvised devices. The ship's obstacles are an engineer's problems: a dead bus, a flat cell, a breaker.

## The ship and the time

- **Days.** Pick of 24 Nov: a fresh disaster. Meals still out, wet blood, systems failing now; the growth is young almost everywhere.
- **A mining ship.** 24 Nov: the engineer joined 'the "Kestrel Vein" project, a astral mining operation that seeks out the stars to harvest rare minerals for Earth and its colonies'.
- Read from these: the rooms are a working ship's rooms, left in the middle of a shift, not a derelict. Old crystal is rare and marks where it began.

## The story

His words of 24 Nov, whole:

> I would like the opening part of the game to feel like a comic being revealed, showing the life the engineer lived before joining the "Kestrel Vein" project, a astral mining operation that seeks out the stars to harvest rare minerals for Earth and its colonies. The idea was to show a decaying city and society that the protagonist seeks to escape by going on the ship, but then things go horribly wrong. My idea was that the first act is the character fighting through the increasingly difficult to escape ship until he eventually hits an escape pod and gets picked up by a merchant ship. However, he has by now become a symbiotic host with the creep, so he becomes a spreader of this plague. So the second act is a story of him coming to terms with being the head of the host - the new brain controlling this super organism. The third act is you willingly conquering the main flag ship of the Earth Merchant federation, where you are destroying the evil that has led you to the place the protagonist is now.

| Part | What happens |
|---|---|
| Opening | A comic being revealed: his life before, a decaying city and society he leaves by joining the ship. |
| Act I | The ship. Each section is harder to get out of. It ends at an escape pod; a merchant ship picks him up. By then he is a symbiotic host. |
| Act II | He comes to terms with being the head of the host, the new brain of the organism. |
| Act III | He willingly takes the flagship of the Earth Merchant Federation and destroys the evil that led him here. |

This outline replaces the three acts of the story team's proposal of 10 Nov (a lost wife, a hidden lunar civilisation, a military AI against the alien collective, a protagonist built by the organism). Which of the proposal's parts survive is open. Until Leon and the story team have settled it, agents write nothing new that depends on Acts II and III of either version, and every text in the game stays a placeholder (G-27).

## What is being built now

- **Section 1 at demo quality** (pick of 24 Nov): one section finished in every lane before content multiplies.
- Act I's sections are **hand-authored and fixed** (pick of 24 Nov). This replaces the hybrid maps of 10 Nov for Act I; seeded variation is kept for contracts mode.
- A section is 10 to 15 minutes for a first-time player (12 Nov).
- The first public release is a free demo of Act I (12 Nov). No public dates (10 Nov).

## Decided look and feel

Not reopened without his word.

- Camera: fixed isometric. Input: gamepad and keyboard with mouse, both from the start.
- Colour: cold blue-grey is the ship; amber is the ship asking for attention; red is alien and nothing else (10 Nov).
- Interface (picks of 20 Nov): menus with CommonUI on UMG; in play a ring at the player's feet (health, stamina, the creep) and prompts beside the thing they belong to. Players of the web alpha found its text too small (10 Nov): text is never small.
- Doors "should open and close dynamically, with a 0.4 second delay after interacting with them" (20 Nov).
- "The rooms seemed bare, they need assets, flavour and the correct interior design respective to the scene at hand" (19 Nov).
- Art: his bought packs for the ship, generated models for what no pack has. Generated art is a placeholder that his designer replaces, the most visible first (10 Nov).
- Text: placeholders by agents, rewritten by the story team; no voice (10 Nov).
- No hunger, thirst or other new survival systems (10 Nov, from the story team's proposal). Taking things apart and making things at workbenches is the fourth line above, not an exception to this.

2. **Where the organism comes from.** The mined mineral? The name and the loadmaster's "mineral samples" point that way; Leon has not said it.
3. **What survives of the story team's proposal:** the wife and her voice, the two intelligences, the lunar civilisation, the three stages of his body.
4. **Section 1's texts** still tell the proposal's version (a survey team's sealed cases, "the Kestrel Vane hangs off the port side", a woman's voice). They wait for 1 to 3 and the story team.

6. **His own infection in Act I:** a hazard only, or the visible beginning of the symbiosis that ends the act?
7. **The opening comic:** who draws it, how long it is, whether the demo has it.
8. **Contracts mode and multiplayer** under the new story. The two reputation sides of 10 Nov came from the old outline.
9. **How hard it is.** The hazard numbers and the darkness of 19 Nov and the creep's numbers of 20 Nov have never been played by a person.
